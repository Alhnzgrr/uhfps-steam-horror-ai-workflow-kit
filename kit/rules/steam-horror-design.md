# Steam Horror Design Rules

## Core Rule

A horror feature is not complete just because it functions. It must support the
intended tension, pacing, exploration, uncertainty, and player vulnerability of
a Steam PC horror game.

## Design Priorities

Evaluate:

- tension buildup
- player uncertainty
- exploration pressure
- sound and silence
- lighting and visibility
- pacing before and after scares
- environmental storytelling
- consequence clarity
- player agency

## Jumpscare Discipline

Do not rely on cheap jumpscares as the main design tool.

Use jumpscares only when they:

- have buildup
- fit the environment
- reinforce the threat
- do not break trust with random noise spam
- leave a meaningful aftermath

## Feature Questions

Before marking a feature ready, answer:

1. What emotion should the player feel?
2. What information is hidden or uncertain?
3. What audio, lighting, animation, or timing supports the moment?
4. Does the mechanic encourage exploration or avoidance?
5. Does the feature respect pause, menu, and input flow?

## Forbidden

- mobile-style reward loops unless explicitly requested
- flashy feedback that weakens horror tone
- constant prompts that remove uncertainty
- puzzle feedback that is unclear in a frustrating way
- scares without pacing or consequence

## Review Questions

- Does this feature increase tension or only add functionality?
- Is the player given enough information to act?
- Is failure readable without becoming comedic or noisy?
- Does the feature fit Steam PC expectations?
